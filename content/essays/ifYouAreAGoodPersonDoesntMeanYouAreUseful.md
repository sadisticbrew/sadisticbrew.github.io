title = 'If You Are A Good Person, Doesnt Mean You Are Useful'

Being a good person is not an achievement. Atleast, not by itself.
As I am growing up, I have started thinking that being a good person and being a useful person are completely different things. Ever since I was little I was taught being a _good_ person is very important in life, but no one teaches that being a good person doesn't automatically solve problems. 

You will not call people who are walking figures of righteousness, justice, honesty, kindness and so on when you are in any kind of problem. You will call them for their usefulness. On their face, you will tell them, "You are a good person, you will be successful." But I think you know what you actually think about them.

Imagine you are in a situation that requires a difficult decision. There are two people you can call. One is incredibly kind, empathetic, honest but has no idea what to do. The other person has solved the same kind of problem ten times. But they are morally terrible, they will lie, deceive, are difficult to deal with, arrogant asshole kind of person. Who will you call? You will probably call the second person even if you avoid them in real life. Such is the human nature. An individual will always choose the best option that the _current_ situation demands, no matter how harshly they dislike the other person.

Goodness is a character trait - it determines what kind of person you are, people will value you for it but they won't see you as a person who is useful. Usefulness on other hand is a track record, it determines what you can do for the world around you. The interesting thing is that society will often try to convince you that being useful is less important that actually being a good person.

This brings me to an interesting question, if nobody is helped by your goodness, what is the value of it?

Are they mutually exclusive? I think not. What would a person who is good but not useful be like? what about the other way around?

To be honest, I would rather be a useful person with a moral compass that is a spinning pinwheel rather than a good person with mediocre usefulness.
